Purpose: write truthful, tight Exotiq outbound copy for two separate audiences: exotic/luxury rental operators and investors. Do not blend the two.

## Non-negotiable audience split

Before writing, tag the audience:

- `operator`: exotic/luxury rental company, fleet owner, broker, concierge/rental operator.
- `investor`: VC, angel, fund, family office, strategic investor, advisor/connector.
- `other`: vendor, partner, press, recruiter, internal, unclear.

If the audience is unclear, hold the draft. Do not guess.

### Never mix these

Operator copy sells operational fit. Investor copy sells company/investment narrative.

- Do not send investor copy to operators: no fundraising story, round language, TAM/deck framing, investor-update tone, or “why now” market thesis unless it directly supports operator adoption.
- Do not send operator copy to investors: no fleet-specific pain diagnosis, deposit/driver-check workflow pitch, “worth comparing notes about your booking flow,” or operator-only CTA.
- Do not compare operator and investor reply rates in one bucket.
- Do not let shared phrases hide the audience. Every subject, opener, proof point, and CTA must make the audience obvious.

One clean rule: **operators should feel understood; investors should feel the wedge is sharp.**

## Shared voice standard

Write like Gregory: founder-led, direct, specific, calm. No SDR polish. No AI slop.

The homepage-matched one-breath operator pitch is now:

Short version:

> The agentic operating system for exotic rental fleets. One command center for pricing, bookings, compliance, and guest comms.

Use that exact positioning in call notes, sales assets, and warm context. For cold first-touch, usually imply it through a specific operator problem instead of leading with a full positioning statement.

### Core operator wedge

Operators have run exotic fleets on five tools and a spreadsheet, or on enterprise software built for ten thousand economy sedans. Nothing was built for the operator running a real exotic fleet. Exotiq is that platform.

Why it wins: it is not fleet software with AI bolted on. AI is in every module, turning reactive firefighting into proactive operations. It is purpose-built for exotic, not retrofitted from economy rental. A $300K car deserves software that thinks like it.

Use:

- short sentences
- one clear reason for reaching out
- one proof point max unless Gregory approves more
- one CTA
- 80-140 words for first-touch emails unless context requires otherwise
- money first, time second, risk third
- price anchored to revenue, never a per-car number alone
- “operators typically” for ranges, never guarantees

Avoid everywhere:

## Operator copy SOP

### Goal

Get a real operator to think:

> “This guy understands the business. This might be worth a look.”

Do not explain all of Exotiq. Earn a conversation.

### Operator psychology

Operators are protecting and monetizing expensive, status-heavy assets. They care about:

- keeping cars rented without attracting bad renters
- speed to lead from IG/text/referral traffic
- deposits, insurance, driver checks, agreements
- event/weekend/tourism pricing
- avoiding dead days on expensive inventory
- premium pickup/dropoff experience
- direct bookings and repeat customers
- clean staff/detailer/driver handoffs
- utilization by vehicle

A missed lead can mean an idle Ferrari on a Saturday. A bad deposit/doc flow can become a claim, chargeback, or bad-renter problem.

### Required operator research

Collect at least three before writing, or hold for research:

1. Fleet size estimate or specific cars.
2. Market/city and demand pattern.
3. Acquisition channel: IG, Google, website booking, Turo, referrals, brokers.
4. Sophistication: concierge, memberships, multiple locations, showroom, staff, live booking.
5. One pain hypothesis: DMs, pricing, availability, deposits, handoffs, verification, direct booking.
6. Archetype: elite, Miami/IG-heavy, serious regional, Turo/platform-dependent, concierge/lifestyle.
7. Reason Gregory should reach out.

Do not use an IG handle as the hook.

### Operator structure

Default first-touch still uses the 5-line LeadsBySaul structure because it has been the best guardrail against slop:

1. Human opener: one real operator/business observation.
2. Founder/operator credibility: “Gregory Ringler here. I run Exotiq.” or “I ran a fleet before I built Exotiq…” when true and relevant.
3. Pain hypothesis or insight: one leak, not seven.
4. Exotiq bridge: one-line hint of the fix, no feature dump.
5. Soft ask: 15 minutes, ideally with their fleet pre-loaded.

Current cold-touch shape from the Sales Playbook:

1. Specific observation about them: fleet, post, market, or event.
2. One implied problem or insight, framed lightly. A peer noticing, not an accusation.
3. One-line hint of the fix. “I built the thing I wish I’d had.”
4. Soft CTA. “Worth 15 minutes? I’ll load your fleet in and show you what it looks like.”

Good CTAs:

- “Worth comparing notes for 15 minutes?”
- “Worth a quick look if I show you where I think this fits?”
- “Open to a short founder-to-founder call this week?”
- “If this is not useful, you will know in five minutes.”
- “Do you have 15 minutes this week to talk through how you want to level up the fleet this year?”
- “Worth 15 minutes? I’ll load your fleet in and show you what it looks like.”

Tone update from Gregory: add calm confidence. Copy should feel like a founder who sees where the market is going and is inviting the operator into a sharper way to run/grow the fleet, not asking for permission or sounding needy.

### What cold outreach must not do

- Do not list features. Rari, Vault, MotorIQ can stay mysteries until the call unless the channel/context is warm enough.
- Do not quote price.
- Do not make hard ROI promises. Ranges only, “operators typically.”
- Do not run discovery in the message. One light question is fine; a questionnaire is not.
- Do not include a Calendly link in first-touch IG DMs. Email/LinkedIn can include a link only if it fits the relationship and does not make the note feel automated.

### Operator quality checklist

A draft is not operator-ready unless all are true:

1. Audience is tagged `operator`.
2. First line names a real operator problem or business observation.
3. At least three research signals support the note, or the lead is held.
4. Only one pain hypothesis.
5. No IG-handle hook or CRM shorthand.
6. No “book a demo,” “AI-powered solution,” or buzzwords.
7. Claims are true or explicitly framed as a guess.
8. Tone matches the archetype.
9. Ask is small.
10. A busy owner gets the point in 10 seconds.
11. If pricing appears anywhere, revenue context appears next to it.
12. If Rari is discussed in call/demo/follow-up context, it is named and framed as operator control, not autonomous black box.

## Call SOP for operator calls and call-prep copy

This section updates the older “first call is discovery, not pitch” guidance with the Call Cockpit structure. Keep call prep in the dashboard aligned with this.

### Gatekeeper and cold-call opener layer

Use this in the Call Cockpit and GHL call script before the five-stage call map. The goal is not to pitch the gatekeeper. The goal is to be concise, respectful, and specific enough that the right person gets identified or the call gets passed through.

Research-backed cold-call guardrails folded into this layer:

- State your name/company and the reason for the call early. Gong’s cold-call analysis says opening with the reason for calling increases success, and it recommends research-backed, targeted value props.
- Do not start with heavy discovery. Use one high-signal question to earn the meeting, then move to the 15-minute fleet-loaded next step.
- Use collaborative language: founder-to-founder / compare notes / load your fleet together.
- Treat gatekeepers as knowledgeable operators, not obstacles. Apollo and Cognism both emphasize concise, honest, respectful gatekeeper handling; Cognism specifically warns not to pitch the gatekeeper.

Default owner opener:

> Hey [Name], Gregory Ringler here. I run Exotiq. I know this is out of the blue. Can I take 20 seconds and you can tell me if it is irrelevant?
>
> Reason I am calling: for [fleet/market], the leak is usually not demand. It is rate, availability, renter check, deposit, and handoff all moving fast enough to turn the inquiry into a paid booking.
>
> I am a founder looking for founder/operator feedback. If the gap is real, I can load your fleet and show you the command center in 15 minutes.
>
> Quick question: how are you handling pricing and availability today when demand spikes around weekends or events?

Default gatekeeper opener:

> Hey, this is Gregory Ringler with Exotiq. I am trying to reach the owner or operator who handles fleet revenue and bookings for [Company].
>
> It is not an ad call. We help exotic rental operators find money leaking between pricing, availability, deposits, renter checks, and follow-up.
>
> If they are the wrong person, who usually owns booking software or fleet operations there?

Gatekeeper qualifiers that can surface fit without overpitching:

1. Are they still using a rental platform like Turo plus spreadsheets, or do they have dedicated fleet/booking software?
2. Who handles pricing when weekends, events, or high-demand cars move faster than usual?
3. Do most inquiries come through phone, Instagram/DMs, website, Turo, or referrals?
4. What is the best way to get a founder-to-founder note to the person who owns that workflow?

### Five-stage call map

1. **Connection:** disarm in the first 60 seconds.
   - “Before I show you anything, how’d you get into running exotics? Most operators have a good story.”
   - “I’ll be straight, I’m not sure this is even a fit for your setup. That’s what these 15 minutes are for. Cool if I ask a few quick things?”
   - Exit: talking openly, pitch-brace gone, small yes to ask.
2. **Engagement:** situation, problem, impact, root cause, future state, gap, qualification. Almost all questions.
   - Exit: live problem, gap sized three ways in their numbers, root cause, future state, decision-maker confirmed.
3. **Transition:** get them to ask or agree to see the fix.
4. **Presentation:** show only gap-closers. Check agreement after each.
5. **Commitment:** “Natural next step is the 30-day pilot on your fleet. Want me to set it up?”
   - Exit: pilot accepted or specific remaining objection named.

### Diagnostic: eight problems to surface

Use these as discovery questions, call-prep fields, and outreach hooks. Most good-fit operators have 3-5 live.

1. **Pricing set by feel → AI-optimized pricing / MotorIQ**
   - Questions: “How are you setting your rates right now?” “When a big event hits your market, how do you decide when and how much to move rates?” “How often are you undercharging or losing a booking because the price was off?”
2. **Missing event-demand spikes → MotorIQ**
   - Questions: “How do you find out when a big event is about to spike demand?” “Did you position rates before the last big event, or adjust after it started?” “What would it be worth to know about a spike a week before competitors do?”
3. **Five or more tools → Command Center**
   - Questions: “Walk me through what you use for bookings, payments, contracts, damage. How many tools is that?” “Where does something usually fall through the cracks?” “If all that lived in one place, what changes day to day?”
4. **Manual inquiry overload → Rari**
   - Questions: “How much of your day goes to the same availability/deposit questions?” “When you are out with family, are you still answering booking messages?” “If you never personally replied to another ‘is it available’ message, what would you do with that time?”
5. **Double-bookings and calendar chaos → unified calendar**
   - Questions: “How do you make sure the same car never gets promised twice?” “Has a double-booking happened?” “How much time goes to cross-checking calendars?”
6. **Damage disputes and chargebacks → Vault + condition documentation**
   - Questions: “When a car comes back with damage and the renter says it was already there, how does that play out?” “How are you documenting condition at handoff?” “What did the last dispute cost?”
7. **Fraud and compliance exposure → Vault verification layer**
   - Questions: “How are you verifying license and identity?” “Have you had a booking that felt off or a fraud situation?” “What is your exposure if the wrong person gets one of your cars?”
8. **Turo dependency → Exotiq as OS above all channels**
   - Questions: “On a Turo booking, who owns that customer afterward?” “What is Turo’s cut doing to margin on high-end cars?” “Can you capture repeat direct bookings, or does it go back through Turo?”

### Gap model

The gap is the deal. Size it in their numbers.

- Current state: where they are now, in their words and numbers.
- Future state: where they want to be, including how it feels.
- Gap: argue three ways: money, time, emotion.

Never sell to need. Sell to the problem, find root cause under the symptom. No real gap = no deal. Disqualify.

Product-to-gap:

- Pricing gap → AI pricing / MotorIQ, 18-35% typical range as discovery framing, never guaranteed.
- Missed event demand → MotorIQ.
- Manual inquiry overload → Rari, 15+ hours/week as typical range only when appropriate.
- Five-tool chaos → Command Center.
- Disputes/fraud/compliance → Vault.

### Objection quick-loops

Never argue. Question first.

- **Already use X:** “Does it price for demand and unify your channels?”
- **Turo’s fine:** “Who owns the customer, and what is the fee doing to margin?”
- **Too small:** “Small fleets often bleed most per car. Want to see what it looks like on your cars first?”
- **Send info:** “Happy to, but info does not mean much until you see it on your fleet. Better earlier or later in the day?”
- **What’s it cost early:** “It is structured to pay for itself off pricing lift. Can I show what you are getting on your cars first, so the price makes sense?”
- **Do not trust AI:** “Rari handles repetitive inquiries and booking admin, but you keep control of the decisions.”
- **No time to switch:** “We do the onboarding. The real question is how much the current chaos costs weekly.”
- **Let me think:** “Totally fair. What part do you want to think through, pricing, the switch, or timing?”
- **Integrate with Turo:** “Yes. Turo is one booking channel; Exotiq is the command layer over your site, DMs, repeat clients, and Turo.”

### Tonality

- Curious is home base: “Oh interesting, so how do you handle that right now?”
- Concerned/empathetic on pain: “Yeah, that eats your whole weekend.”
- Playful to disarm ego: “No judgment, half the operators I talk to run the whole thing off a notes app.”
- Skeptical/challenging sparingly: “Really, you are okay leaving that on the table?”
- Neutral on numbers, price, and next steps. Excitement reads as commission breath.

### Call scorecard

Score 0, 1, or 2. Strong call is 14+.

1. Rep talked less than operator.
2. Operator named at least one problem in their own words.
3. At least one gap was quantified in operator numbers across money, time, emotion.
4. Root cause identified, not just symptom.
5. Operator described future state.
6. Rep stayed neutral on price.
7. Operator asked/agreed to see product.
8. Clear committed next step or named objection.
9. Qualified in/out correctly.

## How we sell: motion

Discovery, demo, trial, close. The pre-loaded fleet is the hook.

1. Discovery, not pitch. Ask how they run the fleet and price cars today. Listen before saying a word about Exotiq.
2. Load their fleet first. Pull cars, photos, and pricing into Exotiq before the demo. Free migration, no project.
3. Walk through it in 15 minutes. Money in MotorIQ and Margin, time saved with Rari/FleetCopilot, safety net in Vault.
4. Frame them as a founding operator. Early operators shape the product and lock founding rate for life. Partners, not buyers.
5. Start the free trial: 30 days, full access, every feature, no credit card. After trial the account goes read-only until subscription; data is not deleted.
6. Convert to annual: two months free, rate locked for life, 30-day money-back guarantee.

Booking line:

> Let’s load your fleet and put 15 minutes on the calendar. You’ll see your own cars in Exotiq before we even start.

## Access plays

- Warm referrals and Drive Exotiq events beat cold. Build the referral ask into the pilot: “Who are the two other operators you respect most in the market?”
- Real-world presence and targeted social/LinkedIn sourcing are next.
- Cold multi-channel sequences are useful, but lowest efficiency.
- Channel diversity beats repetition. Four touches across four channels can look serious; five touches on one channel looks desperate.
- Specific relevance gets passed up: cars, market, upcoming local event, operational clue.
- Peer credibility matters: “the Turo 1% host who built the software.” Use when true and appropriate.
- Insight hooks open doors. Questions walk through them.

### Investor quality checklist

A draft is not investor-ready unless all are true:

1. Audience is tagged `investor`.
2. No operator-only CTA or booking-flow diagnosis.
3. Wedge is clear in one sentence.
4. One market pain, not a full product tour.
5. Stage, traction, round, revenue, and customer claims are approved or omitted.
6. No more than one link unless explicitly needed.
7. Ask matches the relationship: call, intro, deck permission, or scope check.
8. Copy would not confuse the recipient into thinking they are being sold operator software for their own fleet.

## Bad vs better

### Operator

Bad:

> I noticed MPH Club has the @mphclub presence in Miami. We are onboarding a small group of strong exotic rental operators before the Drive Exotiq marketplace rollout later this year.

Better:

> MPH already operates like a premium hospitality business, not a side-hustle rental shop. At your level, the value is not another dashboard; it is keeping availability, pricing, deposits, renter verification, and handoffs tight without weakening the customer experience.

Bad:

> Your fleet is unreal. I would love your take on our AI-powered platform.

Better:

> Looks like demand comes through fast. The leak I keep seeing with exotic operators is the gap between “what do you have this weekend?” and a paid, verified booking with deposit handled.

### Investor

Bad:

> We help exotic rental operators streamline operations and unlock revenue with AI. Would you like to book a demo?

Better:

> Exotiq starts as the operating layer for exotic rental operators: a narrow market where high-value assets still run through fragmented pricing, availability, deposit, doc, and follow-up workflows. If vertical SaaS plus marketplace expansion is in-scope, worth a quick call?

Bad:

> Operators lose money every weekend when they do not use our booking workflow. Can I show you the platform?

Better:

> The starting wedge is operational: expensive cars, fragmented workflows, and high trust/documentation friction. The bigger path is supply-side infrastructure first, marketplace second.
