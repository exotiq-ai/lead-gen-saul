# Ask Saul Voice Agents: Marketing Partner Talk Track

Audience: one sales lead and one marketing / SEO lead at a marketing group that wants to sell Saul voice agents.

Goal of the conversation: help them understand what Saul is, how the demo works, what the customer experience looks like, what they can confidently sell, and where Gregory / Ask Saul handles implementation.

---

## 1. Simple opener

> I appreciate you guys taking the time. The simplest way to think about Saul is this: Saul is an AI phone agent for local businesses that cannot afford to miss calls.
>
> It answers the phone, asks the right intake questions, captures the lead details, and routes the information into the business workflow, usually GHL, CRM, text, email, or a follow-up process.
>
> It is not meant to replace a great office person. It is meant to catch the calls that currently go to voicemail, get missed after hours, or get handled inconsistently.

Key framing:

- Saul helps local businesses stop losing phone leads.
- Saul is strongest for service businesses where phone calls turn into jobs.
- The first version should usually be intake, qualification, and routing, not fully autonomous dispatch or overpromised booking.

## 4. What Saul does on a call

Explain the call flow in five steps.

### Step 1: Saul answers

> Saul answers the call with a natural voice. The customer knows they are talking to an assistant, but the goal is to be helpful and fast, not deceptive.

### Step 2: Saul identifies the caller and need

Saul asks for basics like:

- Name
- Phone number
- Business or property context
- Service needed
- Location or service area
- Urgency
- Preferred timing

### Step 3: Saul qualifies the call

Depending on the business, Saul can ask:

- What happened?
- Is this urgent?
- What city are you in?
- Is this residential or commercial?
- Are you looking for repair, replacement, estimate, or maintenance?
- When would you like someone to follow up?

### Step 4: Saul routes the lead

Saul can route the summary into:

- GHL
- CRM
- SMS
- Email
- Dashboard
- Internal notification workflow
- Follow-up queue

### Step 5: The business follows up with context

> Instead of getting a mystery voicemail, the business gets a structured lead record: who called, what they need, where they are, how urgent it is, and what should happen next.

## 9. What they should not overpromise

This is important. Keep expectations clean.

Do not promise:

- Guaranteed revenue increase
- Guaranteed booked jobs
- Same-day deployment for every customer
- Perfect emergency handling
- Full human replacement
- That Saul can answer every possible question perfectly
- That Saul can directly dispatch technicians without defined rules
- That every CRM integration is instant
- That setup is zero work from the client

Safer language:

- “Saul helps capture missed and after-hours calls.”
- “Saul can ask intake questions and route the lead.”
- “The exact setup depends on the customer’s workflow.”
- “We start with a focused call flow and improve it from real call data.”
- “For higher-risk cases, Saul should collect context and escalate to a human.”

Talk track:

> The biggest mistake would be selling this like magic. It works best when the scope is clear: answer, qualify, capture, route, and escalate. That alone solves a very expensive problem.

## 15. Partner model options

Keep this flexible. Do not overcommit in the first meeting.

### Option A: Referral partner

They introduce interested businesses. Gregory / Ask Saul sells and fulfills. They receive a referral fee or revenue share.

Best if they want low operational burden.

### Option B: Co-sell partner

They position Saul to existing clients. Gregory joins the sales call to explain implementation and close technical details.

Best for early deals.

### Option C: Reseller partner

They sell Saul under their own offer, with Ask Saul handling implementation behind the scenes.

Best later, after the sales process and support model are proven.

### Option D: White-label later

Possible later if volume justifies it, but not necessary for the first few deals.

Talk track:

> I would not overcomplicate the partner model on day one. Let’s prove the offer with a few real businesses first, then decide whether referral, co-sell, reseller, or white-label makes the most sense.

## 17. Common objections and responses

### “Will customers know it is AI?”

> They should. Saul should not pretend to be human. The goal is to be useful, clear, and fast, not deceptive.

### “Can it book appointments?”

> It can, depending on the calendar and rules. For many businesses, the safer first version is to capture the lead and have a human confirm the appointment.

### “Can it handle emergencies?”

> It can identify urgent situations and escalate based on rules, but we need to define those rules carefully. We do not let AI make high-risk promises without guardrails.

### “Does it work with GHL?”

> Yes, GHL is one of the main workflows we are building around. The exact fields, notes, opportunities, and automations depend on the customer’s GHL setup.

### “Is this an answering service replacement?”

> Not exactly. If they have a great human answering service, keep it. Saul is best for missed calls, overflow, after-hours, repetitive intake, and structured lead capture.

### “How fast can this launch?”

> It depends on the complexity. A focused first workflow can move quickly, but we still need call-flow mapping, testing, and routing setup before pushing real callers through it.

### “What if Saul gets something wrong?”

> That is why the first version should focus on intake and routing. Saul collects context and escalates. We tune from real calls over time.
